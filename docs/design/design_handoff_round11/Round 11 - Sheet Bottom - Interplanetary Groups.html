The answer, in one line

The reassurance belongs to the field, not to the sheet. Once it sits under the input, the region under Save holds a single worded exit, the X has no job left, and the stack of four never forms.

Under Save: Save, then Not now. Two items. That is the whole change.

Why the field, and not the sheet

The line answers a question the input raises: what happens to the thing I am about to type. Field help sits under its field in every other part of the product, and a member reading top to bottom meets the promise at the moment they are deciding whether to type. Mounted under Save instead, it arrives after the decision it was meant to inform, and it takes the one position on the sheet where a decline can be found.

Frame B is the honest alternative: Orbit makes the promise in the note, which is right about voice. It loses on placement. On the second ask the note is five sentences long, and a promise appended to that is a sixth sentence nobody reaches. Field-attached keeps the line in the same place in both asks, which is what makes it learnable.

The X

Deleted. Both positions in round 10 were correct, which meant the element was in the wrong place: a bordered circle next to a bordered note competes, and an unbordered glyph is not a control. Frame D moves it to the scrim and proves the styling can be fixed, but it leaves a sheet whose only exit is a symbol, which fails the eighty-year-old test. With the reassurance out of the way, the worded exit fits and the glyph is redundant. Deleting it also removes the round 10 problem entirely rather than restyling it.

What still dismisses

  • Not now — labelled, 17px, weight 600, --text-secondary, underlined at 4px offset, min-height 48px, centred under Save. Spends one of the two lifetime asks.
  • The scrim, and the system back gesture — silent, spends nothing.
  • The grab bar — signals a draggable sheet, and a downward drag is a scrim-class gesture: silent, spends nothing.

Flagging the thing you asked us to flag. The labelled exit and the scrim now look like the same gesture and are not: one costs the member an ask, the other does not. We think that is acceptable and in the right direction, because the silent gesture is the cheap one. A member who taps outside gets asked once more later; a member who deliberately reads and taps Not now has told us something, and spending the ask honours it. It would only be a problem inverted.

The grab bar does do real work, so the worded exit can be quieter than a button: underlined text rather than a bordered secondary. It cannot be quieter than legible. 17px, weight 600, full-width 48px target.

The line itself

“I use it to sign you in and send you plans. I never share it or sell it.”

  • First person, because everything else on the sheet is Orbit speaking. Your draft was in the passive (“Only used to”), which is the register of a policy; the promise is stronger when someone makes it.
  • Two clauses, one line of intent each: what it does, what it will never do. Two sentences, 15 words, no dashes.
  • --type-meta 15px, --text-secondary. Above the 13px floor and clearly subordinate to the 17px copy. Not fine print: fine print would be 13px and grey, and this is a promise, not a disclaimer.
  • It says plans rather than reminders or digest, so it stays true when the digest ships and does not name a feature the member has not seen.

Where it does not go

Not on the code step. By then the address is given and the promise is retrospective; the code step's one supporting line is already the resend, and two subordinate lines under one button is the stack we are avoiding. The code step is unchanged from round 10.

Growth

Frame F is the tallest state at ·. Beyond that, .ea-sheet takes max-height: 100% and .ea-pad scrolls inside it, so enlarged device text lengthens the scroll rather than pushing Save off the page. Nothing clips at any text size. The 65% stays a floor.

CSS delta from round 10

  • .ea-fieldgroup — label, input and .ea-assure as one unit, so the promise moves with the field and never drifts under Save.
  • .ea-assure — 15px, --text-secondary, margin-top: 9px, text-wrap: pretty.
  • .ea-exit — replaces .ea-notnow: same treatment, min-height 48px.
  • .ea-sheet { max-height: 100% } plus a scrolling .ea-pad.
  • .ea-top and .ea-x are no longer used by the recommended sheet. They stay in email-ask.css for other surfaces; delete the markup here, not the class.
  • Frames C, D and E use .ea-closing, .ea-scrim.xtop / .ea-xrow and .ea-x.bare. Those exist to draw the rejected arrangements. Do not ship them.

Nothing depends on hue. Save is the only teal element. Lime appears only inside Orbit's mark.
