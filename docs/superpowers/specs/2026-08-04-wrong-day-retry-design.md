# The wrong-day retry: a second chance for an idea the day blocked

**Date:** 4 August 2026 · **Slice branch:** `feat/wrong-day-retry` · **Status:** approved by the product owner in the brainstorming session of 4 Aug 2026, section by section.

## What this slice is

Today, when beers for Friday closes with two "I'm in" and one "Yes, can't Fri", the goodbye posts and the idea dies, even though three people said yes to beers and only the day failed. This slice gives that idea a second chance without pretending Orbit knows something it does not. At close, instead of the goodbye, Orbit says the day was the only problem and asks what day would work. A member naming a day restarts everything through the existing spark path. Nobody answering earns exactly one deterministic guess the next evening (the same weekday one week out), posted as a real gauge with chips. If that too gets nothing, the idea is truly dead and Orbit never mentions it again.

It is the second deliberate act of the owner's chattier posture (first: the zero-yes bump in the gauge endgame). Every new speak-or-stay-quiet decision it adds gets its line in the build-notes register, per that register's standing rule.

One premise correction, recorded so the next reader is not misled by older prose: CLAUDE.md's open-question framing said a no on the chips "says nothing about which day works." Half true. The third chip ("Yes, can't Fri") already stores yes-to-the-idea, no-to-the-day, per person. Orbit knows *that* the day failed and for how many people; what it lacks is only *which* day would work, and this design gets that fact by asking the group rather than inventing it.

## Settled decisions (do not relitigate)

1. **Eligibility is would-have-cleared.** A closed-short gauge earns the retry when its "I'm in" plus "can't that day" votes together reach three (spark's own flat bar, current members only, matching every other count in the product), with at least one of them a can't-that-day. The owner chose this over any-can't-day-triggers and over a majority-of-answers variant. Reasoning: every retry message is one the whole group sees, so retries should be rare and near-certain to be about an idea people demonstrably want. "Next time" stays a soft no to the idea and counts toward nothing; honest exits stay honest.
2. **The eligibility check runs before the endgame's closure outcomes.** A qualifying gauge gets the ask instead of the goodbye, and instead of the zero-yes silence: a gauge with zero plain yeses but three can't-that-days is the *strongest* retry case (the day blocked everyone), not the weakest, and it would otherwise die silently under the zero-yes rule.
3. **The ask closes and asks in one message.** It replaces the goodbye for this class; there is no goodbye-then-ask pair. Copy is deterministic, composed from stored facts, in Orbit's voice: "Beers didn't happen for Friday, but three of you want it. What day works better?" The count is derived from vote rows, never stored.
4. **A member's answer rides the existing spark path, unchanged.** Whoever names a day gets a fresh gauge on that day with themselves seeded in (only when they named exactly one day; the existing rule). Votes never carry over: a yes to Friday was never a yes to Saturday ("a vote belongs to a proposal, not to an idea," settled in spark part one and untouched here). The old gauge stays closed forever.
5. **An unanswered ask earns one guess, the next evening.** Around 8pm group-local the day after the ask (joining the 8pm bump-timing family and its reasoning: evenings are when someone can check with the person they share a life with). The guess is a real gauge with chips and a tally, proposing the **same weekday one week after the failed day** (Friday fails, the guess is the following Friday: "No takers on a new day yet, so how about beers next Friday?"). The owner chose same-weekday-next-week over adjacent-day and over the placeholder fallback day. Reasoning: it is the least presumptuous guess, it keeps the one preference the group actually expressed (a Friday-shaped plan), and "can't Fri" usually means this Friday, not Fridays. The guess-day rule and its timing are named constants in the same placeholder module as Fri/Sat/7pm/9am/8pm/two-hours, so override-learning replaces the whole family as one decision.
6. **"Answered" means a new gauge opened for the same activity after the ask.** That is what cancels the guess. Unrelated chatter does not cancel it (the ask may simply have been missed), and neither does a new gauge for a *different* activity: if the group pivots to bowling, the beers appetite three people voted for is still real, so the beers guess still fires. Accepted knowingly under the chattier posture; flagged in the debt list as a watch-item because it is the most literal-minded reading.
7. **The loop cap is decided by whose move it was.** A member-named revival is an ordinary gauge with full rights, including earning its own retry later if it fails wrong-day with fresh would-have-cleared votes; each new cycle is bought with fresh votes from real people. Orbit's own guess gauge is the opposite: it is marked as Orbit-guessed and never earns a second automatic cycle, however it dies. No ask on its close, no further guess. The machinery can never ping-pong "next Friday?" down the calendar by itself; human engagement always resets, Orbit alone never does.
8. **The guess gauge is otherwise an ordinary gauge.** Chips, live tally, the eve bump if it earns one (the existing empty-room guard already suppresses the bump when the gauge's own message is still the newest thing in the feed), the two-hour close, promotion at three yeses with RSVPs seeded from votes. Nobody is seeded at birth: Orbit named the day, and Orbit is never a vote.
9. **Time carries across the retry.** A gauge proposes a day, not a time (unchanged). At promotion, the time resolves through the existing ladder using the *original idea's* wording, so "beers at 8" stays an 8pm plan when it lands a week later, and the existing am/pm and fallback disclosure rules apply unchanged. The implementation plan owns the mechanism (likely the guess gauge carrying a pointer to the original gauge or its source message); the product intent is fixed here.
10. **The amendment lands on "one bump, then let it die," and making that edit to CLAUDE.md is the slice's first execution task** (per the standing rules-file rule: a slice whose decision bends a standing rule edits the rule first). The amended shape: one bump remains the allowance for every gauge; a day-blocked idea that would have cleared the bar additionally gets one ask at its close and one guess the evening after, then dies. **"A gauge whose day has passed is dead" is not amended.** It survives untouched, because every revival is a genuinely new gauge; the CLAUDE.md open-question note saying this slice would need to bend it was wrong once the ask-then-guess shape was chosen, and the amendment text should say so.
11. **The chattiness worst case is accepted knowingly: six Orbit messages across roughly ten days** (gauge, eve bump, close-ask, guess gauge, its eve bump, its goodbye), each conditional on the group leaving the previous one hanging. This is the chattier posture doing what the owner asked of it. New register rows: the close-ask (speak), the next-evening guess (speak), the guess gauge's no-second-cycle (stay quiet), and the different-activity-does-not-cancel decision (speak).
12. **Zero new model calls.** The ask and the guess are deterministic, composed from stored facts on Orbit's clock. A member's answer rides the per-message detection the product already pays for. The one model-dependent seam this slice leans on (a bare "Saturday works" after Orbit's ask reading as a fresh beers-on-Saturday idea through the conversational window) gets bench cases before it is trusted, never a walkthrough anecdote.
13. **This slice updates the records it touches, in the same PR:** the CLAUDE.md amendment (first task, decision 10), the register rows (decision 11), the "Where the build is" rewrite at the slice boundary, the build-notes §11 entry, and the pre-deploy checklist entry for whatever migration the markers need.

## Not in this slice (every exclusion names its home)

- **Collecting a preferred day at chip-tap time** ("can't Fri, but Sat works"): belongs to a richer vote UI, if it is ever built at all; the ask gets the same fact conversationally for free, which may make this permanently unnecessary. Not queued.
- **Conflict-aware proposing** (Orbit reasoning about which days conflict for whom): explicitly out of scope, standing; the guess deliberately does not pretend to know what it cannot.
- **Override learning:** still the named successor slice for the whole placeholder family, which this slice grows by one member (the guess-day rule).
- **The pending-events surface and ask-Orbit-what's-open:** unchanged, still the queued rivals for the slice after this one.
- **Verbal RSVP and the both-true claim:** untouched, tracked where CLAUDE.md already tracks them.
- **No change to any existing bump, goodbye, or urgency copy** for gauges that do not qualify; the endgame's behavior for below-bar and zero-yes gauges without a can't-day quorum is exactly as it shipped.

## How this will be verified (written before any code)

**Suite baseline, recorded:** 576 tests across 40 files, all passing, run on this branch on 4 Aug 2026 before any code, exactly matching the endgame slice's recorded finishing number. No pre-existing failure is carried. One known wrinkle to carry honestly: the suite-wide `testTimeout` fix is stashed, uncommitted, awaiting its own micro-PR; until it lands, the two files that have flaked on the 5s default remain able to flake (neither did on the baseline run), and a flake there during execution is the known timeout pattern, not a new failure this slice inherits.

**Automated, against a controlled clock (the injected-clock pattern the sweep already follows), every test written first and watched failing:**

- Eligibility both ways: 2 in + 1 can't-day gets the ask; 2 in + 0 can't-day gets the ordinary goodbye; 0 in + 3 can't-day gets the ask, not the zero-yes silence; 1 in + 1 can't-day (below bar) closes exactly as the endgame shipped. Counts are current-member filtered.
- The ask posts exactly once, under the concurrent-sweep discipline the endgame's review established (create-then-conditionally-attach, proven by deterministic concurrent tests), and replaces rather than accompanies the goodbye.
- The guess fires the next evening only when no same-activity gauge has opened since the ask; a member-named revival suppresses it; a different-activity gauge does not; it never fires twice.
- The guess gauge proposes the same weekday one week out, correct across a month boundary and in a non-UTC group timezone (this slice's new date arithmetic does not join the all-UTC-fixtures debt; at least its own tests pin a real timezone).
- The guess gauge is marked Orbit-guessed, and its close never produces an ask or a second guess, whatever its votes say.
- Promotion of a retry gauge resolves its time from the original idea's wording, with the existing disclosure rules intact.

**Bench, not suite:** new `eval:detect` cases for the answer seam: a bare "Saturday?" and a "Saturday works" following Orbit's ask in context (expected: fresh gauge on Saturday, namer seeded), an ambiguous "next week?" (names no single day; expected to follow the existing exactly-one-day rule rather than silently picking), and a look-alike Orbit must not misread (a nostalgic "Saturday was fun" after the ask). Baseline and after numbers go to build-notes §11, same as the recognition slice.

**By hand, in the browser against dev-test,** staged versus lived stated plainly in the PR: the close-ask rendering for a qualifying gauge; a member answering with a day and the new gauge opening seeded; an unanswered ask followed by a staged next-evening sweep producing the guess gauge with chips; taps on the guess gauge through promotion with the time carried; and the guess gauge dying with no second cycle. The staging extends the committed `scripts/qa-stage-endgame.ts` precedent, with `npm run db:which` first, every time.

## Debt this slice expects to open

- **Two more undated placeholders** (same-weekday-next-week, and the ask-to-guess gap of about a day anchored at 8pm) join the family awaiting override-learning. Medium visibility: both are group-visible messages when wrong.
- **The activity-exact "answered" test is literal-minded.** A group that pivots to a different activity still gets the original guess the next evening. Accepted knowingly; revisit if real QA reads it as pushy.
- **Engineering expectation, flagged not settled:** the gauge record likely grows an ask-message pointer (idempotency), an Orbit-guessed marker, and a link back to the original gauge for time carrying; that likely means a migration, which carries deploy obligations onto the checklist.
- **The cron report's failure undercount, carried from the endgame, now covers more outcomes.** The sweep grows new result kinds while the result union still has no failure variant. Unchanged here, recorded so "carried" stays visible.
- **Chat remains the only surface all of this lands on.** Six worst-case messages is the chattiest one idea has ever been; the queued pending-events surface is the pressure relief, and this slice makes its case stronger.
